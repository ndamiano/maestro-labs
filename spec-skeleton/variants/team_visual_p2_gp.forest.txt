# gameplay.md

## 1. Game Summary

The player is a lone wanderer trapped in a haunted forest at night. The only escape is the Old Gate to the north. The gate is sealed and requires three bone keys. Dawn arrives after 8 real-time minutes. If the player does not cross the gate threshold before dawn, they fail.

A single hostile entity, **The Hollow**, wakes in the central clearing and hunts anyone it sees or hears. The player cannot fight The Hollow. The only solution is to manage light, manage movement, use cover, collect the keys, and reach the gate.

### Core Fantasy

- Explore a dangerous forest.
- Use a lantern to see and create temporary safe space.
- Avoid one readable hunter.
- Escape before time runs out.

### Core Loop

1. Move through the forest.
2. Manage lantern fuel and breath.
3. Use cover and light to avoid The Hollow.
4. Collect three bone keys.
5. Reach the Old Gate.
6. Open the gate and cross the threshold before dawn.

## 2. Dimensionality

### Decision: 2D Top-Down

The game is a **2D top-down** game with a fixed north-up camera.

- The camera follows the player.
- North is always up.
- The camera zoom is static.
- Any visual layering from the visual designer is cosmetic only. All gameplay uses one 2D tile grid.

### Why 2D Top-Down

1. **Readable tension.**  
   Light radius, noise radius, enemy sight, and cover are easier to understand in a top-down view.

2. **Smaller content scope.**  
   A 3D forest would require huge environmental content and complex camera work. A 2D map is easier to make complete and balanced.

3. **Clear pathing and avoidance.**  
   The player needs to understand where The Hollow can and cannot go. A top-down map makes that clear.

4. **Better use of cover.**  
   Trees, underbrush, and clearings can function as readable tactical spaces.

### Why Not 2.5D or 3D

- 2.5D camera tilt can help atmosphere, but it adds complexity without adding meaningful gameplay value.
- 3D would make light, noise, line of sight, and enemy detection much harder to communicate.
- The core experience is stealth, timing, and navigation, which work well in 2D.

### Camera Requirement

- The camera should keep the player centered.
- The view should be wide enough to see the player’s light radius and nearby cover.
- Target readable view size: approximately **24 tiles wide by 16 tiles tall**.

## 3. End-to-End Game Structure

The game is a single-player, single-run experience.

### Game States

| State | Description |
|---|---|
| `Playing` | Normal gameplay. Timers, movement, pickups, and The Hollow are active. |
| `Won` | The gate is open and the player has crossed the safe threshold before dawn. |
| `Failed` | Dawn has arrived, or The Hollow has touched the player. |
| `Retry` | Resets the entire run. |

### Initial State

When gameplay starts:

- Player is in the Start Clearing.
- Dawn timer: **480 seconds**.
- Lantern fuel: **50 / 100**.
- Breath: **100 / 100**.
- Keys collected: **0 / 3**.
- The Hollow: **Sleeping**.
- Gate: **Closed**.

### Win Condition

The player wins when:

1. The gate has been opened, and
2. The player’s center crosses the gate threshold, and
3. Dawn has not yet arrived.

### Fail Conditions

The player fails when either:

1. Dawn timer reaches **0** before the win condition is met.
2. The Hollow’s hitbox overlaps the player’s hitbox.

### Frame Priority

If a win condition and a fail condition happen on the same frame:

- **Win takes priority.**

### Retry

- Pressing retry starts a completely new run.
- All pickups reset.
- The Hollow returns to sleeping.
- Dawn timer resets to 480 seconds.
- The gate resets to closed.

### Why No Checkpoints

The game is designed to be a short, self-contained run. Full retries preserve time pressure and keep the scope small.

## 4. Space and Layout

### Map Overview

The game uses one handcrafted map.

- Map size: **120 tiles wide by 80 tiles tall**.
- Orientation: north is up.
- The map is fully connected.
- No procedural generation.
- No multiple levels.
- All required objectives are on this one map.

### Why One Handcrafted Map

1. Guarantees all cases are testable.
2. Allows precise placement of keys, fuel, cover, and The Hollow.
3. Prevents unfair generated layouts.
4. Keeps the game complete and polished.

### Tile Types

| Tile Type | Passable | Movement Effect | Cover | Blocks Sight/Sound |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|
| Path / Grass | Yes | Normal speed | No | No |
| Underbrush | Yes | 85% speed | Yes, when lantern is off | No |
| Tree Trunk / Rock / Water | No | Impassable | N/A | Yes |

### Movement Rules

- Player can move in 8 directions.
- Diagonal movement is normalized so it is not faster than straight-line movement.
- The player cannot pass through tree trunks, rocks, or water.
- The Hollow cannot pass through tree trunks, rocks, or water.
- Underbrush slows movement but does not block line of sight or sound.

### Key Coordinates

Using the 120x80 map:

- `x` increases east.
- `y` increases south.
- `y = 0` is the northern border.

### Ember Stone Coordinates

There are five ember stones.

### Zone Layout

The map is divided into functional zones.

  - Safe introduction before The Hollow wakes.

#### 2. Southeast Root Shrine

- Location: `(95, 60)`
- Content:
  - Bone Key A.
  - Ember 1.
  - Small clearing with stone features.
  - Some underbrush for cover.
- Purpose:
  - First key.
  - Teaches key pickup, fuel pickup, and noise from interaction.

#### 3. Central Clearing

- Location: `(60, 40)`
- Size: approximately 18x18 tiles.
- Content:
  - Bone Key C.
  - Ember 4.
  - Dead Well at `(60, 40)`.
  - Open exposure.
  - The Hollow wakes here.
- Purpose:
  - Highest-risk objective area.
  - Forces the player to decide when to enter and when to leave.

#### 4. Western Thorn Grove

- Location: `(22, 26)`
- Size: approximately 14x12 tiles.
- Content:
  - Bone Key B.
  - Ember 2.
  - More underbrush than other zones.
  - Narrow tree cover.
- Purpose:
  - Rewards stealth and cover use.
  - Provides a safe hiding opportunity before the final gate run.

#### 5. Northeast Fuel Hollow

- Location: `(88, 22)`
- Size: approximately 8x6 tiles.
- Content:
  - Ember 3.
  - Short branch off the main route.
- Purpose:
  - Optional fuel.
  - Encourages slight route choice without adding a full side quest.

#### 6. North Gate Approach

- Location: around `(60, 10)`
- Size: approximately 10x8 tiles.
- Content:
  - Ember 5.
  - Old Gate at `(60, 5)`.
  - At least two underbrush cover pockets.
- Purpose:
  - Final escape area.
  - Gives the player a chance to refuel before opening the gate.

### Underbrush Coverage

The map should contain at least **8 underbrush clusters**.

Each cluster should be:

- 3 to 4 tiles wide.
- Large enough for the player to hide.
- Placed near key approaches, central clearing routes, and gate approach.

### Route Length Targets

The actual paths should wind through dense forest. The following are target route lengths, not straight-line distances.

| Route | Target Path Length |
|---|---:|
| Start to Key A | 120 to 160 tiles |
| Key A to Key C | 120 to 160 tiles |
| Key C to Key B | 160 to 200 tiles |
| Key B to Gate | 160 to 200 tiles |
| Total expected route | 550 to 750 tiles |

### Why These Route Lengths

- The expected full route should take at least **110 seconds** even for a very fast player.
- This prevents the player from simply beating The Hollow’s 90-second wake time.
- With stealth, fuel management, and enemy avoidance, expected completion should be **5:30 to 7:30**.
- The 8:00 dawn limit should feel tense but fair.

### Layout Rules

1. All keys, embers, and the gate are reachable without first collecting other required items.
2. No corridor should be narrower than 2 tiles.
3. The central clearing must be open enough to make light defense useful, but large enough that The Hollow can approach from multiple sides.
4. The gate approach must have at least two cover pockets.
5. The map must have no required backtracking after the third key if the player follows the compass.
6. The map boundary is impassable except through the gate.

## 5.1 Time and Dawn

### Dawn Timer

- Total time: **480 seconds**.
- The timer counts down in real time.
- The player can win at any time before dawn.
- If the timer reaches 0 before the player wins, the run fails.

### Why 8 Minutes

- Long enough for one full exploration run.
- Short enough to avoid repetition.
- Creates constant pressure without needing a meta-progression system.

## 5.2 Player Movement and Breath

### Controls

| Input | Action |
|---|---|
| WASD / Arrow Keys | Move. |
| Hold Shift or Space | Sprint. |
| L | Toggle lantern on/off. |
| E | Interact with gate. |

### Movement Speed

| Mode | Speed |
|---|---:|
| Walk | 3.6 tiles/sec |
| Sprint | 6.0 tiles/sec |
| Underbrush modifier | 85% speed |

Example:

- Walk in underbrush: `3.6 * 0.85 = 3.06 tiles/sec`.
- Sprint in underbrush: `6.0 * 0.85 = 5.1 tiles/sec`.

### Breath System

Breath controls sprinting.

| Value | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Max breath | 100 |
| Starting breath | 100 |
| Sprint drain | 20 per second |
| Walking recovery | 15 per second |
| Standing recovery | 30 per second |

Rules:

- Sprint is allowed as long as breath is greater than 0.
- If breath reaches 0, sprinting is disabled until breath reaches 25.
- Sprinting drains breath.
- Walking recovers breath.
- Standing still recovers breath faster than walking.

### Why Breath Exists

- Prevents the player from outrunning The Hollow indefinitely.
- Makes sprinting a tactical choice.
- Adds tension to chase moments without adding a full health system.

### No Player Health

The player has no health bar.

- If The Hollow touches the player, the run fails.
- There is no damage, healing, or invulnerability.

### Why No Health

- Keeps the fail state clear.
- Makes The Hollow a serious threat.
- Avoids adding another meter to manage.
- Fits the short, high-stakes escape format.

## 5.3 Lantern and Light

### Lantern State

The player can toggle the lantern on or off.

### Fuel

| Value | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Max fuel | 100 |
| Starting fuel | 50 |
| Ember pickup | +40 fuel |
| Fuel cap | 100 |
| Fuel drain | 0.5 per second while lit |

There are **5 ember stones** on the map.

Total available fuel if all embers are collected:

```text
50 starting fuel + 5 * 40 ember fuel = 250 fuel
```

At 0.5 fuel/sec, this supports:

```text
250 / 0.5 = 500 seconds of continuous light
```

The run is 480 seconds. This means a player who uses all fuel and never turns the lantern off has a small spare margin. If one ember is missed, the player must use darkness for a meaningful portion of the run.

### Light Radius

If the lantern is on and fuel is greater than 0:

```text
light_radius = 4 + (fuel * 0.04)
```

Examples:

| Fuel | Light Radius |
|---:|---:|
| 1 | 4.04 |
| 25 | 5.00 |
| 50 | 6.00 |
| 100 | 8.00 |

If the lantern is off or fuel is 0:

```text
light_radius = 0
```

### Player Visibility

The player’s own visibility is affected by the lantern.

| Lantern State | Condition | Player Visible Range |
|---|---|---:|
| On | Fuel > 0 | `light_radius + 2` |
| Off | Player covered | 3 tiles |
| Off | Player uncovered | 6 tiles |

A player is considered **covered** if:

- The player’s center is in underbrush, or
- The player’s center is adjacent to an impassable tile.

### Light Safe Zone

The Hollow cannot enter the player’s light safe zone under these conditions:

- Lantern is on.
- Fuel is 25 or higher.
- The tile is within the player’s light radius.

If fuel drops below 25, the safe zone disappears.

The light radius still exists below 25 fuel, but it no longer repels The Hollow.

### Why Light Is Central

Light serves three roles:

1. **Navigation.**  
   The player needs it to see the forest.

2. **Defense.**  
   The Hollow cannot enter the safe light radius while fuel is high enough.

3. **Risk.**  
   Using light drains fuel and makes the player more visible.

This creates the core tension: light helps you, but it also spends your escape resource.

### Fuel Warning

The HUD should show a clear warning state when fuel is below 20.

## 5.4 Pickups and Objectives

### Bone Keys

There are **3 bone keys**.

- Keys can be collected in any order.
- Keys are required to open the gate.
- Keys are auto-picked-up when the player overlaps them.
- Picking up a key produces a noise event with radius **6 tiles**.

### Ember Stones

There are **5 ember stones**.

- Ember stones are auto-picked-up.
- Each adds **40 fuel**.
- Fuel is capped at 100.
- Picking up an ember produces a noise event with radius **4 tiles**.

### Gate

The gate is at `(60, 5)`.

- The gate is closed at the start.
- The player can interact with the gate only when all 3 keys have been collected.
- If keys are missing, the gate shows a locked prompt.
- With all 3 keys, the player must hold the interact action for **2 seconds** to open the gate.
- Opening the gate produces a noise event with radius **12 tiles**.
- Once open, the gate remains open.
- The player must cross the threshold to win.

### Gate Threshold

- The threshold is the 3-tile-deep zone where `y < 6`.
- Once the gate is open, The Hollow cannot enter the threshold.
- The player wins when their center enters `y < 6`.

### Why Auto-Pickups

Keys and embers do not require an interact prompt.

- Keeps movement fluid.
- Avoids fumbling during tense moments.
- Still creates noise through the pickup event.
- Only the final gate requires a held interaction because it is the climactic risk.

## 5.5 The Hollow

The Hollow is the only enemy.

### Basic Rules

- The Hollow cannot be killed.
- The Hollow cannot be permanently disabled.
- The Hollow can be avoided, delayed, or kept away using light and cover.
- If The Hollow touches the player, the run fails.

### Hitboxes

| Entity | Hitbox Radius |
|---|---:|
| Player | 0.5 tiles |
| The Hollow | 0.6 tiles |

The Hollow’s visual representation may be larger, but gameplay hitboxes should be slightly smaller than the visual silhouette to avoid perceived unfair contact.

### Wake Time

The Hollow wakes **90 seconds** after gameplay starts.

Wake location:

- Dead Well at `(60, 40)`.

Wake sequence:

1. Sleeping until 90 seconds.
2. Waking state for 8 seconds.
3. After waking, The Hollow becomes Curious.

During Waking:

- The Hollow does not move.
- The Hollow does not detect the player.
- The Hollow does not harm the player.
- A clear visual and audio cue should occur.

### Why 90 Seconds

- Gives the player a safe tutorial period.
- Teaches movement, lantern, sprint, and objective arrow.
- Still early enough that The Hollow is active for most of the run.
- The map is tuned so the player cannot realistically win before this time.

## 5.6 The Hollow State Machine

The Hollow uses a finite state machine.

### States

| State | Description |
|---|---|
| Sleeping | No threat before wake time. |
| Waking | 8-second telegraph. Non-hostile. |
| Curious | Patrols around the wake point. |
| Investigating | Moves to the last known player location. |
| Searching | Searches around the last known player location. |
| Hunting | Actively chases the player. |

---

### State: Sleeping

- Duration: until 90 seconds.
- Movement: none.
- Detection: none.
- Damage: none.

---

### State: Waking

- Duration: 8 seconds.
- Location: Dead Well.
- Movement: none.
- Detection: none.
- Damage: none.
- Purpose: telegraph The Hollow’s activation.

After Waking:

- The Hollow enters Curious.

---

### State: Curious

- Speed: **2.4 tiles/sec**.
- Behavior:
  - Patrols within a 6-tile radius of the wake point.
  - Chooses new waypoints every 2 seconds.
  - Avoids light safe zones.
- Transition:
  - If The Hollow detects the player, it enters Investigating.

---

### State: Investigating

- Speed: **3.2 tiles/sec**.
- Duration: up to 8 seconds.
- Behavior:
  - Moves to the player’s last known position.
  - If it detects the player, it enters Hunting.
  - If it reaches the last known position and does not detect the player, it enters Searching.
- Light rule:
  - If the last known position is inside the player’s light safe zone, The Hollow targets the nearest tile outside the safe radius.

---

### State: Searching

- Speed: **3.2 tiles/sec**.
- Duration: **12 seconds**.
- Behavior:
  - Searches around the last known player position.
  - Chooses random waypoints within a 6-tile radius.
  - Avoids light safe zones.
  - If it detects the player, it enters Hunting.
  - If 12 seconds pass without detection, it returns to Curious.

### Why Searching Matters

- Gives the player a chance to escape by hiding.
- Prevents The Hollow from permanently chasing the player forever.
- Makes stealth meaningful.
- Rewards using cover and turning off the lantern.

---

### State: Hunting

- Speed: **5.0 tiles/sec**.
- Behavior:
  - Targets the player’s current position if detected.
  - If the player is not detected for 6 seconds, targets the last known position.
  - If detection continues, The Hollow remains in Hunting.
  - If the player is lost for 6 seconds, The Hollow enters Searching.
- Light rule:
  - If the player is inside the light safe zone, The Hollow targets the nearest tile outside the safe radius and waits.
  - If the safe zone shrinks or disappears, The Hollow recalculates its path.

### Speed Comparison

| Entity | Speed |
|---|---:|
| Player walk | 3.6 tiles/sec |
| Player sprint | 6.0 tiles/sec |
| The Hollow hunt | 5.0 tiles/sec |
| The Hollow hunt in underbrush | 4.25 tiles/sec |
| Player sprint in underbrush | 5.1 tiles/sec |

### Why These Speeds

- The player can outrun The Hollow while sprinting.
- Breath limits how long the player can outrun it.
- Underbrush slows both entities, but the player can still escape if they have breath.
- The Hollow is fast enough to create real pressure.

## 5.7 The Hollow Perception

The Hollow detects the player through sight and hearing.

Detection is not random.

---

### Sight

The Hollow can see the player if:

1. There is line of sight.
2. The player is within The Hollow’s sight range.

Line of sight:

- Blocked by tree trunks, rocks, and water.
- Not blocked by underbrush.
- Underbrush affects cover state, not ray blocking.

Sight range:

| Player Lantern | Player Condition | Sight Range |
|---|---|---:|
| On | Any | `light_radius + 2` |
| Off | Covered | 3 tiles |
| Off | Uncovered | 6 tiles |

### Why Lantern On Increases Sight

A lit player is easier to see. This makes light both a defensive tool and a liability.

### Why Covered Range Is Lower

When the lantern is off and the player is in underbrush or adjacent to cover, The Hollow has a much harder time spotting them.

---

### Hearing

The player generates noise.

Noise is heard if:

1. The Hollow is within the noise radius.
2. There is line of sight between The Hollow and the player.
3. Noise is blocked by the same impassable tiles that block sight.
4. Underbrush does not block noise.

### Noise Radii

| Action | Noise Radius |
|---|---:|
| Walking | 5 tiles |
| Sprinting | 10 tiles |
| Sprinting in underbrush | 12 tiles |
| Key pickup | 6 tiles |
| Ember pickup | 4 tiles |
| Gate opening | 12 tiles |

### Why Noise Exists

- Movement is a risk.
- Sprinting is powerful but loud.
- Pickups are useful but can attract The Hollow.
- The final gate opening is a deliberate loud event.

---

## 5.8 The Hollow and Light Safe Zones

### Safe Zone Rule

If:

- The player’s lantern is on, and
- Player fuel is 25 or higher, and
- The tile is within the player’s light radius,

then The Hollow cannot enter that tile.

### If The Hollow Is Already in a Tile That Becomes Safe

- The Hollow may remain in that tile.
- It cannot enter new safe tiles.
- It can move out of the safe area if that is allowed by its path.

This prevents The Hollow from being permanently trapped inside the player’s light.

### If the Player Stands in Light

- The Hollow will wait outside the safe radius.
- Fuel continues to drain.
- Once fuel drops below 25, the safe zone disappears.

### Why This Is Fair

- The player can defend with light, but cannot stall forever.
- The Hollow cannot cheat through light.
- The player must eventually move because dawn is approaching.

## 5.9 Compass and Objective Aid

The game includes a subtle objective arrow.

### Objective Sequence

1. Find the first bone key.
2. Find the remaining bone keys.
3. Escape before dawn.

### Arrow Behavior

- Before all keys are collected, the arrow points to the nearest uncollected bone key.
- After all keys are collected, the arrow points to the gate.
- The arrow appears when the objective target is off-screen.
- The arrow is hidden when the target is visible on-screen.
- The arrow is not a minimap.
- The arrow does not show obstacles.

### Arrow Algorithm

1. Determine the objective target:
   - Nearest uncollected key, or
   - Gate if all keys are collected.
2. Use A* pathfinding to find the path from the player to the target.
3. If a path exists, point the arrow toward the first meaningful waypoint.
4. If no path exists due to an error, fall back to straight-line direction.
5. Update the arrow twice per second.

### Why a Compass Arrow Exists

- Prevents soft-stuck states.
- Reduces frustration.
- Still requires the player to navigate manually.
- Avoids giving away the full map.

### Why No Minimap

A minimap would remove too much tension and make the forest feel less like an unknown space.

## 6. Algorithms

## 6.1 Movement and Collision

### Player Movement

- Use continuous movement on the tile grid.
- Normalize the input vector so diagonal movement is not faster.
- Apply tile collision.
- Impassable tiles block movement.
- Underbrush slows movement by multiplying speed by 0.85.

### The Hollow Movement

- Use continuous movement on the tile grid.
- The Hollow follows waypoints generated by pathfinding.
- The Hollow’s movement speed is modified by underbrush.
- The Hollow cannot enter impassable tiles.
- The Hollow cannot enter active light safe tiles.

---

## 6.2 Line of Sight

Use a grid raycast algorithm, such as DDA or Bresenham.

### Sight Ray

- Cast a ray from The Hollow to the player.
- If the ray hits a blocking tile, line of sight is blocked.
- Blocking tiles:
  - Tree trunk
  - Rock
  - Water
- Underbrush is not blocking for sight.

### Cover Rule

The player is considered covered if:

- The player’s center tile is underbrush, or
- The player’s center tile is adjacent to a blocking tile.

Cover only reduces The Hollow’s sight range when the player’s lantern is off.

---

## 6.3 Noise Detection

Noise is checked every frame.

### Continuous Noise

While the player is moving:

- If walking, use noise radius 5.
- If sprinting, use noise radius 10.
- If sprinting in underbrush, use noise radius 12.

### Event Noise

Pickups and gate interaction create one-time noise events.

| Event | Noise Radius |
|---|---:|
| Key pickup | 6 |
| Ember pickup | 4 |
| Gate open | 12 |

If The Hollow is within the noise radius and has line of sight, the player is detected.

### Detection Priority

If multiple detections occur:

- The newest detection becomes the last known position.
- If the player is currently visible, The Hollow targets the player directly.
- If the player is not visible, The Hollow targets the last known position.

---

## 6.4 Pathfinding

Use A* on the tile grid.

### Neighbor Rules

- 8-directional movement.
- Impassable tiles are invalid.
- Active light safe tiles are invalid for The Hollow.

### Tile Costs

| Tile Type | Cost |
|---|---:|
| Path / Grass | 1.0 |
| Underbrush | 1.25 |
| Impassable | Infinite |
| Light safe tile | Infinite for The Hollow |

### Recalculation

- The Hollow recalculates its path every **0.5 seconds**.
- The Hollow also recalculates immediately when:
  - It changes state,
  - The player enters or exits a light safe zone,
  - The player’s fuel drops below the safe threshold,
  - The player is detected in a new location.

### If No Path Exists

If The Hollow cannot path to its target:

- Move to the nearest accessible waypoint.
- Wait or locally wander near the last reachable position.
- Do not teleport.
- Do not path through blocking tiles.

### Why This Matters

The Hollow must feel smart but not unfair. It should be able to navigate the forest, respect cover, and react to light without cheating.

---

## 6.5 Compass Pathfinding

The compass arrow uses the same A* system.

- Target: nearest uncollected key, or gate.
- Frequency: twice per second.
- If the target is off-screen, display the arrow.
- If the target is on-screen, hide the arrow.
- If pathfinding fails, use straight-line direction.

---

## 6.6 Timer Systems

### Dawn Timer

```text
dawn_timer = 480 - elapsed_time
```

If:

```text
dawn_timer <= 0
```

and the player has not won, the run fails.

### Fuel Drain

```text
if lantern_is_on:
    fuel -= 0.5 * delta_time
```

Clamp fuel between 0 and 100.

### Breath Drain and Recovery

```text
if sprinting and breath > 0:
    breath -= 20 * delta_time
else:
    if standing:
        breath += 30 * delta_time
    else:
        breath += 15 * delta_time
```

Clamp breath between 0 and 100.

Sprinting is disabled if breath is 0 until breath reaches 25.

---

## 6.7 Win and Fail Check Order

Each frame:

1. Update player movement.
2. Update The Hollow movement.
3. Check win condition.
4. Check fail by The Hollow contact.
5. Check fail by dawn.

This means if the player crosses the threshold and a fail condition happens on the same frame, the player wins.

---

## 7. Progression and Feel

## 7.1 Progression Structure

The game has three phases.

### Phase 1: Safe Start

Time: `0:00 to 1:30`

Objective:

- Learn movement.
- Learn lantern.
- Learn sprint and breath.
- Reach Key A.

The Hollow is sleeping.

The player should feel:

- Curious.
- Slightly tense.
- Not yet in immediate danger.

---

### Phase 2: The Hollow Wakes

Time: `1:30 to approximately 5:30`

Objective:

- Collect remaining keys.
- Manage fuel.
- Avoid The Hollow.
- Use cover and light.

The Hollow is active.

The player should feel:

- Pressured.
- Cautious.
- Aware of noise and light.

---

### Phase 3: Escape

Time: approximately `5:30 to 8:00`

Objective:

- Reach the gate.
- Open the gate.
- Cross the threshold.

The final moment should feel:

- Fast.
- Risky.
- Resolved.

---

## 7.2 Expected Timing

Target completion times:

| Player Skill | Expected Time |
|---|---:|
| Efficient | 5:30 to 6:30 |
| Cautious | 6:30 to 7:30 |
| Hard fail threshold | 8:00 |

### Why These Times

- The player should feel the possibility of failure but not the feeling of impossible timing.
- A cautious player should have enough time if they manage fuel and avoid bad fights.
- A reckless player should fail from noise, bad light use, or bad routing.

---

## 7.3 Feel Goals

The game should feel:

- Slow at first.
- Increasingly tense after The Hollow wakes.
- Dangerous in the central clearing.
- Quiet in underbrush.
- Desperate near the gate.

### Key Feel Rules

1. The player should usually have 2 or 3 meaningful choices:
   - Turn lantern on or off.
   - Walk or sprint.
   - Hide or move.

2. The Hollow should never feel random.
   - It should always be reacting to sight or noise.

3. The player should usually understand why they died:
   - They made too much noise.
   - They used too much light.
   - They stayed in the open.
   - They ran out of breath.
   - They ran out of fuel.
   - They were too late.

---

## 8. HUD

The HUD should be minimal.

## 8.1 HUD Elements

| HUD Element | Location | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Dawn timer | Top-left | Shows time remaining until failure. |
| Moon/sun dial | Top-left | Shows progression from night to dawn. |
| Objective text | Top-center | Current goal. |
| Key slots | Top-center | Shows 0 to 3 collected keys. |
| Fuel bar | Top-right | Shows lantern fuel from 0 to 100. |
| Breath bar | Top-right, below fuel | Shows breath from 0 to 100. |
| Objective arrow | Edge of screen | Points to current objective when off-screen. |
| Interaction prompt | Near player | Shows gate interaction when relevant. |

---

## 8.2 Dawn Timer

- Display as `mm:ss`.
- Shows time remaining until dawn.
- Should enter a clear warning state when below 60 seconds.
- The moon/sun dial should move toward dawn as time decreases.

---

## 8.3 Objective Text

The objective text should be short.

Examples:

- `Find the bone keys`
- `Find the remaining bone keys`
- `Escape before dawn`

When at the gate:

- If keys are missing:  
  `The gate is sealed (x / 3)`
- If all keys are present:  
  `Hold to open the gate`

---

## 8.4 Key Slots

Display three key slots.

- Empty slots show uncollected keys.
- Filled slots show collected keys.
- This gives immediate progression feedback.

---

## 8.5 Fuel Bar

- Shows fuel from 0 to 100.
- Should have a clear warning state when fuel is below 20.
- The player needs to know when safe-zone defense is about to disappear.
- Safe zone disappears below 25 fuel, so the warning should start early.

---

## 8.6 Breath Bar

- Shows breath from 0 to 100.
- Should be smaller or secondary to the fuel bar.
- Should clearly show when sprinting is unavailable.
- No warning state is required below a threshold, but the bar should communicate that sprint is locked when breath is 0.

---

## 8.7 Objective Arrow

- Appears at the edge of the screen.
- Points toward the nearest uncollected key or the gate.
- Hidden when the target is visible on-screen.
- Should be subtle enough not to feel like a minimap.

---

## 8.8 Required Cues

The following gameplay events require clear visual or audio cues. The visual/audio designer will create the actual assets.

| Event | Required Cue |
|---|---|
| The Hollow wakes | Clear visual and audio cue. |
| The Hollow enters Hunting | Clear state-change cue. |
| Fuel below 20 | HUD warning. |
| Dawn below 60 seconds | Timer warning. |
| Key picked up | Pickup feedback. |
| Ember picked up | Fuel increase feedback. |
| Gate ready to open | Interaction prompt. |
| Gate opened | Successful escape prompt. |
| Player caught by The Hollow | Fail-state feedback. |
| Dawn arrives | Fail-state feedback. |

---

## 8.9 What Is Not in the HUD

The HUD does not show:

- Minimap.
- Health bar.
- Sanity meter.
- Enemy position.
- Fuel threshold number.
- Breath threshold number.
- Inventory list.
- Quest log.

### Why

The HUD should communicate only the information needed for survival:

- Time remaining.
- Fuel.
- Breath.
- Objective progress.
- Current goal direction.

Everything else is meant to be learned through play.

## 10. Edge Cases and Rules

## 10.1 Player Runs Out of Fuel

- Lantern light disappears.
- Player sees only ambient range:
  - 3 tiles if covered.
  - 6 tiles if uncovered.
- The Hollow no longer respects light safe zones.
- The player can still complete the run, but it is harder.

### Why

Running out of fuel should be risky, not automatically fatal. It forces the player to rely on cover and stealth.

---

## 10.2 Player Runs Out of Breath

- Sprinting is disabled until breath reaches 25.
- The player can still walk.
- The Hollow can catch the player if they cannot escape.

### Why

Breath failure should create escape tension, not an instant fail state.

---

## 10.3 Player Stands in Light Safe Zone

- The Hollow waits outside the safe radius.
- Fuel continues to drain.
- Once fuel drops below 25, the safe zone disappears.
- The Hollow can then enter.

### Why

This prevents permanent stalling while still allowing defensive light use.

---

## 10.4 The Hollow Cannot Path to the Player

- The Hollow moves to the nearest accessible waypoint.
- It waits or locally wanders.
- It does not teleport.
- It does not pass through blocking tiles.

### Why

The Hollow must feel like a physical entity, not a camera lock.

---

## 10.5 Player Hides in Underbrush

- If the lantern is off and the player is covered, The Hollow’s sight range is 3 tiles.
- The Hollow can still hear the player if the player makes noise within 5 tiles, or 12 tiles if sprinting in underbrush.
- If The Hollow loses the player during Searching, it leaves after 12 seconds.

### Why

Hiding should be viable, but it should not be a free reset.

---

## 10.6 Player Reaches Gate Without All Keys

- Gate remains closed.
- The player cannot win.
- The player can wait, but dawn will eventually fail them.

### Why

Keys are required progression.

---

## 10.7 Player Opens Gate but Does Not Cross

- The gate remains open.
- The player can still cross later.
- Dawn still applies.
- The Hollow still applies outside the threshold.

### Why

Opening the gate is a major progress moment, but the player still needs to commit to escape.

---

## 10.8 The Hollow Is in the Threshold When the Gate Opens

If The Hollow is inside the gate threshold when the gate opens:

- Move The Hollow to the nearest tile outside the threshold.
- Do not damage The Hollow.
- Do not count this as a fail unless The Hollow is still overlapping the player before the win check.

### Why

This prevents an unfair last-tile interaction.

---

## 10.9 Win and Fail Happen on the Same Frame

- Win takes priority.

### Why

This prevents a frustrating feel where the player crossed the threshold but failed anyway.

---

## 10.10 Player Ignores the Compass

- The player can choose any route.
- The compass points to the nearest objective.
- The player is not forced into a single path.
- If the player makes poor routing choices, dawn becomes the fail state.

### Why

The game should preserve player agency while still preventing soft-stuck states.

---

## 10.11 Player Wastes Fuel

- If the player keeps the lantern on too long, fuel may run out.
- The game does not punish this with a separate fail state.
- The punishment is reduced visibility and loss of safe-zone defense.

### Why

This keeps resource management meaningful without adding another meter.

---

## 10.12 Player Sprints Too Much

- Sprinting drains breath.
- If breath runs out, the player cannot escape by sprinting.
- Sprinting also makes the player easier to hear.

### Why

Sprinting should be a tool, not a permanent escape method.

---

## 11. Design Cuts and Logic

These are intentionally excluded.

### No Sanity Meter

**Cut.**

Why:

- Light fuel and breath already create tension.
- A sanity meter would add a third resource to manage.
- It could create ambiguous fail states.
- The player should understand failures clearly: time, noise, light, or The Hollow.

---

### No Health System

**Cut.**

Why:

- One contact with The Hollow should be dangerous.
- A health bar would reduce horror tension.
- It would add unnecessary complexity.
- The short run works better with a clear fail state.

---

### No Weapons

**Cut.**

Why:

- The fantasy is escape, not combat.
- Weapons would change the core loop from stealth to fighting.
- It would require enemy damage, health, attack cooldowns, and balancing.

---

### No Multiple Enemies

**Cut.**

Why:

- One enemy is enough to create tension.
- Multiple enemies would require more AI, more sound cues, and more player attention.
- The Hollow is more readable as a single hunter.

---

### No Crafting

**Cut.**

Why:

- The game is only 8 minutes long.
- Crafting would add unnecessary inventory management.
- The core loop is already complete with keys, fuel, and breath.

---

### No Upgrades

**Cut.**

Why:

- There is no meta progression in a single run.
- The player should not get stronger; the player should learn to avoid danger.

---

### No Procedural Map

**Cut.**

Why:

- A handcrafted map guarantees balance.
- It allows precise placement of keys, fuel, cover, and The Hollow.
- It ensures the game is complete for all cases.

---

### No Checkpoints

**Cut.**

Why:

- The game is short.
- Full retries preserve pressure.
- Checkpoints would add save-state complexity without improving the core experience.

---

### No Minimap

**Cut.**

Why:

- It would reduce the unknown-forest feeling.
- The compass arrow gives enough guidance.
- Navigation is part of the tension.

---

### No Side Quests

**Cut.**

Why:

- The objective is simple: collect three keys and escape.
- Side content would dilute the pressure of dawn.
- The game is complete without it.

---

### No Lore Notes

**Cut.**

Why:

- Lore would not affect the core loop.
- The player can infer the danger from the environment and audio.
- Adding lore risks turning the game into a collect-a-thon instead of an escape.

---

## 12. Final Design Summary

The game is a complete 2D top-down haunted forest escape.

The player:

- Starts in a safe clearing.
- Has 8 minutes until dawn.
- Must collect 3 bone keys.
- Must manage lantern fuel and breath.
- Must avoid one enemy, The Hollow.
- Must reach the Old Gate.
- Wins by crossing the gate threshold before dawn.
- Fails by dawn or by being touched by The Hollow.

The design is focused on:

- Readable stealth.
- Time pressure.
- Light as a resource and liability.
- Breath as an escape limit.
- One clear enemy behavior system.
- A short, complete, end-to-end experience.
