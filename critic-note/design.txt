Build this 2D top-down fish-eating game where you start as a tiny reef fish and grow by consuming smaller fish while avoiding larger ones, reaching a final size to win. Every number below is a given value to write into the code as-is; none of them needs checking or deriving.

RECORDS: PLAYER, FISH, SPECIES, OBSTACLE, PARTICLE.

PLAYER: x and y are float coordinates in world pixels, starting at (480, 360). vx and vy are float velocity in pixels per second, starting 0. size is a float starting at 8, representing the fish's body length in pixels. speed is a float in pixels per second, starting at 140. alive is boolean, starting true. score is an integer starting at 0. level is an integer starting at 1, representing the current size tier. facing is a float angle in radians.

FISH: species_id is an integer indexing into the SPECIES roster. x and y are float world coordinates. vx and vy are float velocity in pixels per second. size is a float sampled from the species' min_size and max_size range at spawn. speed is a float in pixels per second, sampled from the species' base_speed with ±15% variation. state is one of "wander", "flee", "chase", "idle". state_timer is a float in seconds counting down the current state's duration. flee_x and flee_y are float target coordinates when fleeing. wander_x and wander_y are float target coordinates when wandering. alive is boolean.

SPECIES: a fixed roster of 10 entries. Each carries name (string), min_size and max_size (floats in pixels), base_speed (float pixels per second), color (hex string), flee_distance (float pixels), chase_distance (float pixels), points (integer score awarded when eaten), and tier (integer 1 through 5, controlling which size tiers of the player can encounter this species). The roster is: Guppy, 5, 8, 90, #7ec8e3, 60, 0, 5, 1. Tetra, 7, 11, 110, #e37e7e, 70, 0, 10, 1. Clownfish, 10, 16, 100, #f5a623, 80, 0, 20, 2. Angelfish, 14, 22, 95, #9b59b6, 90, 0, 30, 2. Pufferfish, 18, 28, 70, #8bc34a, 100, 0, 50, 3. Parrotfish, 24, 36, 85, #2ecc71, 110, 0, 75, 3. Barracuda, 35, 55, 130, #7f8c8d, 130, 140, 120, 4. Moray Eel, 45, 70, 80, #d35400, 140, 150, 180, 4. Shark, 65, 100, 120, #34495e, 160, 170, 300, 5. Whale, 110, 160, 60, #1a5276, 200, 210, 500, 5.

OBSTACLE: x and y are float world coordinates of the center. radius is a float in pixels. kind is one of "rock", "coral", "kelp_cluster". These are placed by the map generator and block movement.

PARTICLE: x and y are float world coordinates. vx and vy are float velocity in pixels per second. lifetime is a float in seconds counting down. max_lifetime is a float. type is one of "bubble", "eat_burst", "death_poof", "ring". color is a hex string.

SYSTEMS: PLAYER_MOVEMENT, FISH_AI, COLLISION_AND_GROWTH, SPAWNING, MAP_GENERATOR, CAMERA.

PLAYER_MOVEMENT: WASD and arrow keys both set the player's target direction. Holding a direction for at least 0.05 seconds sets vx and vy toward that direction at the player's current speed. Releasing all keys sets vx and vy to 0 over 0.15 seconds of deceleration. The player's facing angle updates to match the current velocity vector whenever speed exceeds 10 pixels per second. On touch screens, a virtual joystick in the lower-left quadrant of the screen maps thumb offset to the same direction vector, with the same speed. The player's x and y update each frame by vx times dt and vy times dt. If the player's next position would place the player circle (radius size times 0.4) overlapping an OBSTACLE circle (radius), the player slides along the obstacle edge: the component of velocity pointing into the obstacle is zeroed, the tangential component is preserved. The player cannot leave the world bounds of 0 to 1200 in x and 0 to 900 in y; clamping applies.

FISH_AI: Each FISH evaluates its state every 0.2 seconds. If a FISH's state_timer reaches 0, it transitions. In "wander" state, the fish picks a new wander_x and wander_y within 200 pixels of its current position, clamped to world bounds, and swims toward that point at its speed for 2 to 5 seconds. In "flee" state, the fish swims away from the threat point (flee_x, flee_y) at speed times 1.4 for 1.5 to 3 seconds. In "chase" state, the fish swims toward the nearest smaller fish within chase_distance at speed times 1.2 for up to 4 seconds. In "idle" state, the fish has vx and vy of 0 for 0.5 to 1.5 seconds, then transitions to wander. Transition rules: if the player's size is greater than or equal to the fish's size plus 2, and the player is within the fish's flee_distance, the fish enters "flee" with flee_x and flee_y set to the player's position. If a fish's size is greater than or equal to the player's size plus 2, and the player is within the fish's chase_distance, the fish enters "chase" targeting the player. If a fish's size is greater than or equal to another FISH's size plus 2, and that other fish is within chase_distance, the fish enters "chase" targeting that fish. If no threat or target exists, the fish returns to "wander" after a 0.3 to 1 second "idle". Fish collide with obstacles using the same slide rule as the player. Fish bounce off world bounds by negating the relevant velocity component.

COLLISION_AND_GROWTH: Each frame, check circle overlap between the player (radius size times 0.4) and every alive FISH (radius size times 0.4). If they overlap and the player's size is greater than or equal to the fish's size plus 2, the fish is eaten: the fish's alive becomes false, the player's score increases by the fish's species points, and the player's size increases by the fish's size times 0.12 (minimum 0.5 added). A PARTICLE of type "eat_burst" spawns at the collision point with 6 to 10 sub-particles radiating outward at 80 to 150 pixels per second, lifetime 0.4 seconds, colored with the eaten fish's species color. If they overlap and the fish's size is greater than or equal to the player's size plus 2, the player is eaten: the player's alive becomes false, a "death_poof" PARTICLE spawns, and the game-over screen triggers. If sizes are within 2 of each other, neither is eaten; they simply push apart by nudging each other 3 pixels along the collision normal. When the player's size crosses a tier threshold (tier 1: 8 to 15, tier 2: 16 to 25, tier 3: 26 to 40, tier 4: 41 to 65, tier 5: 66 to 100), the player's level increments, a "ring" PARTICLE pulses outward from the player over 0.6 seconds, and the player's speed increases by 10 pixels per second. The player wins when size reaches 100.

SPAWNING: At the start of each level, spawn 20 FISH of species whose tier is less than or equal to the player's current level plus 1. Fish spawn at random positions at least 250 pixels from the player and at least 30 pixels from any OBSTACLE. When the total alive FISH count drops below 12, spawn 5 additional FISH at the edges of the world (within 60 pixels of a boundary), choosing species weighted toward the player's current level. Weighting: species at the player's level get weight 3, species at level minus 1 get weight 2, species at level plus 1 get weight 1. Spawn interval: check every 2 seconds. The Whale species only spawns when the player is level 5. The Shark species only spawns when the player is level 4 or 5.

MAP_GENERATOR: A seeded generator places the world contents. Seed is an integer. The world is 1200 by 900 pixels of open water. Place 14 OBSTACLEs: 6 rocks with radius 20 to 45, 5 coral with radius 15 to 30, 3 kelp_clusters with radius 25 to 40. Positions are random but must be at least 120 pixels apart from each other and at least 150 pixels from the player start (480, 360). Place 40 decorative bubble particles at random positions that drift upward at 20 pixels per second and respawn at the bottom when they leave the top. VERIFIER: no OBSTACLE overlaps another; no OBSTACLE is within 150 pixels of (480, 360); all OBSTACLEs are within world bounds with their radius. Re-roll with the next seed if any fails.

CAMERA: The viewport is 960 by 720 pixels. The camera centers on the player with a lerp factor of 0.08 per frame. When the player's size is below 30, the zoom is 1.0. Between 30 and 60, zoom is 0.85. Between 60 and 80, zoom is 0.7. Above 80, zoom is 0.6. The zoom transition lerps over 1 second. The camera clamps so the viewport never shows outside the 1200 by 900 world.

CORE LOOP: The player swims through the reef, hunting fish smaller than itself while dodging larger predators. Every few seconds a fish is eaten, the size bar creeps up, and new fish spawn to keep the reef populated. Crossing a tier threshold triggers a level-up that opens encounters with new species and slightly faster movement. The pressure escalates: at level 1 you dodge clownfish; at level 4 barracudas hunt you; at level 5 a whale patrols the far corners. The win condition is reaching size 100, which requires roughly 80 to 120 successful eats across five tiers. Losing all lives (one life, no continues) sends you back to the title. The score rewards eating higher-tier fish and chaining eats within 2 seconds of each other (a 1.5x multiplier on the second and subsequent chain hits).

SCREENS: title (game name, a slowly swimming loop of three AI-generated fish sprites, "Press SPACE or tap to start") → game (HUD visible, play begins) → game over (shows final score, size reached, level reached, "Press SPACE or tap to restart") → win (shows "You became the apex predator!" with final score and time elapsed, "Press SPACE or tap to play again"). SPACE or a tap on any non-game screen advances to the next state. Escape during game pauses and shows a small overlay with "Resume (SPACE)" and "Quit (Q)". The HUD shows: top-left a size bar (a horizontal bar from 0 to 100 filled proportionally to PLAYER.size, with tick marks at tier thresholds 15, 25, 40, 65), top-right score as an integer and current level as "Lv N", and a small chain multiplier indicator near the score when active. The player's fish sprite is drawn at PLAYER.x, PLAYER.y scaled by size divided by 8.

AUDIO: Generated with the Web Audio API. A soft looping ambient pad (low filtered noise, 80 Hz to 200 Hz bandpass, volume 0.15) plays continuously during the game screen. "Eat" sound: a short pitched pop (sine wave, frequency proportional to the eaten fish's size, 200 Hz to 600 Hz, 0.1 seconds, volume 0.4) triggered on COLLISION_AND_GROWTH eat. "Death" sound: a descending glissando (sawtooth, 400 Hz to 80 Hz over 0.8 seconds, volume 0.5) triggered when the player is eaten. "Level up" sound: an ascending arpeggio of three sine notes (523, 659, 784 Hz, 0.1 seconds each, volume 0.35) triggered on tier threshold crossing. "Chain" sound: a quick high ping (sine, 900 Hz, 0.08 seconds, volume 0.3) triggered when the eat chain multiplier activates. Bubble ambience: random soft "bloop" sounds (sine, 300 to 500 Hz, 0.06 seconds, volume 0.1) triggered every 1 to 3 seconds by the decorative bubble particles.
