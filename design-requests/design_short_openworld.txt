Build this browser top-down squirrel game in which you run around a small neighborhood, collect high-quality nuts, and beat a rival raccoon to the day's nut point target. It is made of these systems. Every number below is a given value to write into the code as-is; none of them needs checking or deriving.

SYSTEMS: LEVEL RECORD, NEIGHBORHOOD GENERATOR, PLAYER RECORD, SQUIRREL MOVEMENT, NUT RECORD, NUT GENERATOR, NUT QUALITY, RACCOON RECORD, RACCOON AI, COLLISION AND PICKUP, SCORE AND TIMER, PROGRESSION, WIN AND LOSE, CONTROLS, SCREENS, AUDIO, ART.

LEVEL RECORD: The Level record is the source of truth for one neighborhood and has fields index, seed, width, height, pixelWidth, pixelHeight, tileSize, targetPoints, daySeconds, raccoonCount, nutSpawnRate, houseCount, treeCount, pondCount, roadCount, fenceCount, tile, burrow, oakTree, playerSpawn, and raccoonSpawns. The tile field is a grid of width by height Tile records, and each Tile record has fields x, y, type, solid, centerX, and centerY, where x and y are tile coordinates, type is one of grass, road, house, fence, tree, pond, burrow, or oakTree, and centerX and centerY are pixel centers. The burrow and oakTree are single Center records with fields centerX, centerY, width, and height, where width and height are tile counts and centerX and centerY are pixel centers. The playerSpawn is a single Point record with fields x and y, and the raccoonSpawns are a list of Point records with fields x and y, all pixel coordinates. The Neighborhood Generator writes the Level record, and the Squirrel Movement, Nut Generator, Raccoon AI, Collision and Pickup, Score and Timer, and Win and Lose systems read it directly instead of copying its geometry or stats.

NEIGHBORHOOD GENERATOR: The Neighborhood Generator is seeded with Level.seed and fills the Level record in this order: first it fills every tile with grass, then it places roadCount road strips, then houseCount houses, then fenceCount fence loops, then treeCount trees, then pondCount ponds, then the burrow, then the oakTree, then playerSpawn, then raccoonCount raccoonSpawns. A road strip is a straight 3-tile-wide line of road tiles with length 8 to 14 tiles, and a house is a 4-by-3 block of house tiles. A fence loop is a closed 1-tile-thick rectangle with perimeter 20 tiles of fence tiles, a tree is a 1-by-1 tree tile, and a pond is a 2-by-2 block of pond tiles. The burrow is a 2-by-2 block of walkable burrow tiles, the oakTree is a 2-by-2 block of solid oakTree tiles, playerSpawn is a walkable tile at least 2 tiles in Manhattan distance from the burrow and at least 12 tiles in Manhattan distance from the oakTree, and each raccoonSpawn is a walkable tile at least 10 tiles in Manhattan distance from playerSpawn. It writes Tile.type and Tile.solid for every placed tile, writes the burrow and oakTree Center records, and writes playerSpawn and raccoonSpawns. The generator uses the seed to choose positions, orientations, and lengths, and it never writes content by hand. The verifier checks that no two placed blocks overlap, that no road strip crosses another road strip, that no fence loop self-intersects or crosses another fence loop, that playerSpawn, burrow, oakTree, and every raccoonSpawn are on walkable tiles or adjacent to a walkable tile, that playerSpawn is at least 12 tiles in Manhattan distance from the oakTree, that every raccoonSpawn is at least 10 tiles in Manhattan distance from playerSpawn, that every walkable tile is reachable from playerSpawn by 4-way movement, that the tile grid contains at least Level.houseCount connected house blocks, Level.treeCount tree tiles, Level.pondCount connected pond blocks, Level.fenceCount fence loops, and Level.roadCount road strips, and that at least 32 tiles are walkable; re-roll with the next seed if any fails.

PLAYER RECORD: The Player record is the source of truth for the squirrel and has fields x, y, speed, facing, points, and radius. Its initial x and y come from Level.playerSpawn, speed is 180, facing is 0, points is 0, and radius is 12. The Squirrel Movement system writes x, y, and facing; the Collision and Pickup system writes points; and the Score and Timer, Win and Lose, and Raccoon AI systems read Player.points and Player.x, Player.y without keeping a second copy.

SQUIRREL MOVEMENT: The Squirrel Movement system reads the Input record and updates Player.x and Player.y by Player.speed times the frame time, with Player.speed given as 180 pixels per second. The input direction is the Input up, down, left, right, touchX, and touchY fields combined. If the input direction has both horizontal and vertical parts, the direction is normalized before movement. The system tries the new x position first and then the new y position, and it rejects any position where the Player circle of radius 12 would intersect a solid tile in the Level tile field, using Level.tileSize 32, or leave the Level.pixelWidth by Level.pixelHeight bounds. The facing field is set to the last nonzero input direction, and if there is no input the squirrel does not move.

NUT RECORD: The Nut record is the source of truth for each nut and has fields id, x, y, type, points, active, lifetime, and radius. Its id is the next integer from 1, its x and y are pixel centers, its type is one of the Nut Quality names, its points come from the Nut Quality table, its active flag starts true, its lifetime starts at 25 seconds, and its radius is 10. The Nut Generator writes new Nut records and decrements lifetime; the Collision and Pickup system writes active and reads points; and the Score and Timer system reads only the Player and Raccoon point records for score display.

NUT GENERATOR: The Nut Generator is seeded with Level.seed and a spawn clock, and it places nuts by rolling positions rather than using a hand-written layout. At level start it spawns 12 nuts immediately, and then it attempts one spawn every 1 second. On each attempt, if the number of active nuts is less than 24 and a random value from 0 to 1 is less than Level.nutSpawnRate, it chooses a walkable Tile from the Level tile field, uses that Tile.centerX and Tile.centerY as the nut position, and rejects the position if it is within 48 pixels of Player.x and Player.y, within 48 pixels of any Raccoon x and y, within 24 pixels of another active Nut, or inside a solid tile. It re-rolls the position up to 4 times before giving up. It selects the Nut type from the Nut Quality weights, writes a new Nut record, and sets lifetime to 25 seconds; when a Nut lifetime reaches 0, the Nut Generator sets active to false.

NUT QUALITY: The Nut Quality system defines the Nut type records with fields type, points, weight, and tier. The roster is acorn with 5 points, weight 50, and common tier; walnut with 10 points, weight 30, and common tier; hazelnut with 15 points, weight 12, and fine tier; black walnut with 25 points, weight 6, and rare tier; and golden acorn with 50 points, weight 2, and best tier. The Nut Generator uses these weights to choose a type, and the golden acorn is excluded from the first 30 seconds of a level. When the Collision and Pickup system collects a nut, it adds the Nut type's points to the collector's points, and the golden acorn is the best nut in the game.

RACCOON RECORD: The Raccoon record is the source of truth for each rival raccoon and has fields id, x, y, speed, points, state, targetX, targetY, cooldownUntil, and radius. Its initial x and y come from Level.raccoonSpawns, speed is 150, points is 0, state is patrol, targetX and targetY are its current position, cooldownUntil is 0, and radius is 14. The Raccoon AI system writes x, y, state, targetX, targetY, and cooldownUntil; the Collision and Pickup system writes points; and the Score and Timer and Win and Lose systems read Raccoon.points and Raccoon.x, Raccoon.y without keeping a second copy.

RACCOON AI: The Raccoon AI system updates each Raccoon every frame and re-chooses its target every 0.5 seconds, processing Raccoons in Raccoon.id order. If Raccoon.state is flee, it moves directly away from Player.x and Player.y at Raccoon.speed until Raccoon.cooldownUntil passes, then it sets state to patrol. If Raccoon.state is not flee, it searches for the nearest active Nut within 300 pixels; if it finds one, it sets state to seek and sets targetX and targetY to that Nut's x and y, and if it finds none, it sets state to patrol and chooses a random walkable Tile within 200 pixels as targetX and targetY. It then moves toward targetX and targetY at Raccoon.speed, using the same solid-tile and bounds collision as the squirrel, with a circle of radius 14. The Raccoon AI system does not collect nuts itself; the Collision and Pickup system handles raccoon collection, and the Raccoon AI system clears a collected Nut as its target on the next 0.5 second tick.

COLLISION AND PICKUP: The Collision and Pickup system processes player pickups, then raccoon pickups, then raccoon steals, then the oak tree win, and it checks distances using the radius fields in the Player, Nut, and Raccoon records and the center fields in Level. If the distance between Player.x and Player.y and a Nut's x and y is 22 pixels or less and the Nut's active flag is true, the system sets the Nut's active flag to false, adds the Nut's points to Player.points, and plays the pickup sound or the golden sound if the Nut type is golden acorn. If the distance between a Raccoon's x and y and a Nut's x and y is 24 pixels or less and the Nut's active flag is true, the system sets the Nut's active flag to false, adds the Nut's points to Raccoon.points, sets that Raccoon's state to patrol, and plays the raccoonCollect sound. If the distance between Player.x and Player.y and a Raccoon's x and y is 26 pixels or less and the Raccoon's state is not flee, the system reduces Player.points by 15, never below 0, sets the Raccoon's state to flee, sets Raccoon.cooldownUntil to the current time plus 2.0 seconds, and plays the steal sound. If the distance between Player.x and Player.y and Level.oakTree.centerX and Level.oakTree.centerY is 40 pixels or less and Player.points is at least Level.targetPoints, the system triggers a level win.

SCORE AND TIMER: The Timer record is the source of truth for the day clock and has fields timeLeft and levelIndex. The Timer starts with timeLeft equal to Level.daySeconds and levelIndex equal to Level.index, and it decreases timeLeft by the frame time each frame. There is no separate score record; the HUD reads Player.points for the player score, reads the highest Raccoon.points value as the rival score, reads Level.targetPoints for the goal, and reads Timer.timeLeft for the remaining time. The HUD is drawn directly from the source records: the player score and target are drawn in the top-left corner with a 16-pixel margin, the rival score is drawn below them, and a 240-pixel-wide timer bar is drawn in the top-center with a 16-pixel margin. The Score and Timer system does not change points itself; it only displays the existing Player and Raccoon records and reports the timer to Win and Lose.

PROGRESSION: The Progression system loads one of five Level records, each with the fields listed in the Level record, and the level roster is Level 1 with width 24, height 16, pixelWidth 768, pixelHeight 512, seed 13, targetPoints 100, daySeconds 150, raccoonCount 1, nutSpawnRate 0.9, houseCount 6, treeCount 14, pondCount 3, roadCount 4, fenceCount 4; Level 2 with width 28, height 18, pixelWidth 896, pixelHeight 576, seed 7932, targetPoints 140, daySeconds 150, raccoonCount 2, nutSpawnRate 0.8, houseCount 7, treeCount 16, pondCount 4, roadCount 5, fenceCount 5; Level 3 with width 32, height 20, pixelWidth 1024, pixelHeight 640, seed 15851, targetPoints 180, daySeconds 160, raccoonCount 2, nutSpawnRate 0.7, houseCount 8, treeCount 18, pondCount 4, roadCount 5, fenceCount 6; Level 4 with width 36, height 22, pixelWidth 1152, pixelHeight 704, seed 23770, targetPoints 220, daySeconds 165, raccoonCount 3, nutSpawnRate 0.65, houseCount 9, treeCount 20, pondCount 5, roadCount 6, fenceCount 7; and Level 5 with width 40, height 24, pixelWidth 1280, pixelHeight 768, seed 31689, targetPoints 260, daySeconds 170, raccoonCount 4, nutSpawnRate 0.6, houseCount 10, treeCount 22, pondCount 5, roadCount 6, fenceCount 8. After a level win, the Progression system loads the next Level index, and after Level 5 it triggers the final win.

WIN AND LOSE: The Win and Lose system evaluates the player win before any lose condition. A level win occurs when the Collision and Pickup system reports that Player.points is at least Level.targetPoints and the Player is within 40 pixels of Level.oakTree.centerX and Level.oakTree.centerY. A level lose occurs when Timer.timeLeft reaches 0 before a level win, or when any Raccoon.points reaches Level.targetPoints before a level win. On a level win for Level 1 through 4, the system goes to the level complete screen; on a level win for Level 5, it goes to the final win screen. On a level lose, it goes to the game over screen with the reason set to either the day ending or the raccoon reaching the target, and the game over screen offers a retry of the same Level index.

CONTROLS: The Input record is the source of truth for player input and has fields up, down, left, right, touchX, touchY, and touchActive. The player's controls are the keyboard keys W, A, S, and D, and the arrow keys Up, Left, Down, and Right, with both sets bound to the same directions: W and Up set up true, A and Left set left true, S and Down set down true, and D and Right set right true. On touch, the player touches anywhere in the play area and drags; touchX and touchY store the vector from the initial touch point to the current touch point, its length is clamped to 1, touchActive is true while the touch is held, and releasing the touch sets touchActive false and touchX and touchY to 0. A tap on the 48-pixel-square pause icon whose top-left corner is 16 pixels from the top edge and 16 pixels from the right edge opens paused. There is no mouse control.

SCREENS: The Screen record is the source of truth for the current screen and has fields name, levelIndex, and reason. The state machine is title (shows the game title, a squirrel, and a Start button) → levelIntro (shows the level number, targetPoints, daySeconds, raccoonCount, and a Start button) → playing (shows the neighborhood, Player, Nuts, Raccoons, and HUD) → levelComplete (shows Player.points, Timer.timeLeft, and a Continue button) → finalWin (shows the final victory and a Title button) or playing → gameOver (shows the lose reason and a Retry button). In title, Enter or tapping Start leads to levelIntro for Level 1, and Escape does nothing. In levelIntro, Enter or tapping Start leads to playing, and Escape leads to title. In playing, Escape or tapping the pause icon leads to paused. In paused (shows Paused, Resume, and Quit), Enter, Escape, or tapping Resume leads to playing, and Q or tapping Quit leads to title. In levelComplete, Enter or tapping Continue leads to levelIntro for the next Level index, and Escape leads to title. In finalWin, Enter or tapping Title leads to title, and Escape does nothing. In gameOver, Enter or tapping Retry leads to levelIntro for the same Level index, and Escape leads to title.

AUDIO: All audio is generated with the Web Audio API, and the Sound record has fields id, waveform, frequencies, duration, volume, and trigger, with each frequency entry having fields start and end and duration applying per frequency entry. The sound roster is ui, a sine wave with one frequency entry with start 660 and end 880, duration 0.05 seconds, volume 0.2, played when a button is pressed; pickup, a square wave with one frequency entry with start 440 and end 660, duration 0.08 seconds, volume 0.25, played when the player collects a non-golden nut; golden, a triangle wave with one frequency entry with start 880 and end 1320, duration 0.25 seconds, volume 0.35, played when the player collects a golden acorn; raccoonCollect, a sine wave with one frequency entry with start 160 and end 120, duration 0.12 seconds, volume 0.2, played when a raccoon collects a nut; steal, a sawtooth wave with one frequency entry with start 220 and end 80, duration 0.2 seconds, volume 0.3, played when a raccoon steals points from the player; tick, a square wave with one frequency entry with start 1000 and end 1000, duration 0.03 seconds, volume 0.15, played when Timer.timeLeft crosses 10, 9, 8, 7, or 6 seconds; win, a sine wave with three frequency entries, each with start and end equal to 523, 659, and 784 respectively, duration 0.12 seconds, volume 0.3, played on a level win; finalWin, a sine wave with four frequency entries, each with start and end equal to 523, 659, 784, and 1046 respectively, duration 0.18 seconds, volume 0.35, played on the final win; and gameOver, a sawtooth wave with one frequency entry with start 330 and end 110, duration 0.5 seconds, volume 0.3, played on a level lose.

ART: The Art asset record has fields name, kind, and source. Ask for a squirrel as a sprite, a raccoon as a sprite, an acorn as a sprite, a walnut as a sprite, a hazelnut as a sprite, a black walnut as a sprite, a golden acorn as a sprite, a grass tile as a tile, a road tile as a tile, a house tile as a tile, a fence tile as a tile, a tree tile as a tile, a pond tile as a tile, an oak tree as a sprite, a burrow as a sprite, and a title scene as a scene. Each image holds one subject, so the squirrel, raccoon, each nut type, each tile type, the oak tree, the burrow, and the title scene are separate images, and no image is a sheet, atlas, grid, strip, or set of several things. Everything else — HUD text, timer bar, score numbers, movement direction indicator, collision flash, pickup particles, raccoon cooldown outline, level intro background, pause icon, pause overlay, game over overlay — is drawn in code.
