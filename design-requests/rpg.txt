Build this top-down 2D role-playing game. It is made of these systems.

SYSTEMS: Area, Player, Camera, Collision, NPC, Dialogue, Item, Inventory, Equipment, Enemy, Encounter, Battle, Quest, Shop, Progression, Save, Screens, Audio.

AREA: the world is four areas, and every one of them is a tile grid of 32 px tiles laid down by a seeded generator — nothing is hand-drawn. An area is a record: id, name, width and height in tiles, a tile array, a spawn point, a list of exits, a list of NPC ids present, an encounter table id, and a music id. A tile is a record: kind, walkable, and an optional colour variation index so a field of grass is not one flat colour. An exit is a record: a rectangle of tiles, the target area id, and the target spawn point, so walking onto an exit swaps the area and places the player at the far side of the matching exit. Every generator takes the world seed plus the area id, so reloading the game rebuilds the same world. The four generators:

- Meadowbrook (town), 40×30: a generator that lays a grass field, a cobblestone plaza in the centre, a dirt road from the south edge to the plaza and from the plaza to the east edge, four building footprints placed by the generator on non-overlapping plots off the plaza with a door tile each (inn, shop, elder's house, chapel), a fountain of four tiles at the plaza's middle, and fences and flower beds on leftover plots. The south road's edge tile is the exit to the Greenwood; the east road's edge is the exit to the Hollow Road. No encounters here.
- Greenwood (forest), 60×50: a generator that fills the area with grass, then scatters tree clusters by a noise walk — pick a random tile, grow a cluster of 3 to 12 trees outward, repeat until about a third of the area is trees — then guarantees connectivity by carving a winding dirt path from the north exit to the south exit and from that path to a clearing that holds a shrine tile. A pond of water tiles somewhere off the path, water is solid. North edge exit back to town; south edge exit into the Old Mine. Encounter table: forest.
- Old Mine (cave dungeon), 50×50: a generator that starts fully rock, places 7 to 10 rectangular rooms of 4×4 to 9×9 by rejection sampling so they do not overlap, and joins each room to the next by an L-shaped corridor, so every room is reachable. The first room holds the entrance exit back to the Greenwood; the last room holds the boss encounter trigger tile and, behind the boss, a chest. Two other rooms, chosen by the generator, hold a chest each. Torch tiles are placed on room walls every few tiles and give the room a warm light radius; corridors are dark outside a small radius around the player. Encounter table: mine.
- Hollow Road (wilderness), 70×20: a long east–west strip, a generator that lays a dirt road down the middle with grass either side, hills as solid tiles in ridges the road bends around, a ruined tower footprint of solid tiles with one door tile near the east end, and dead trees. West edge exit back to town. The tower door is an interior exit into a single generated 12×12 tower room that holds the final chest. Encounter table: road.

Every generator ends by verifying with a flood fill from the spawn that every exit, chest, shrine and NPC tile is reachable, and re-rolls its own placements with the next seed if not.

PLAYER: position in pixels, facing (one of four directions), speed 160 px/s, a current area id, and the stats record: level, xp, max hp, hp, max mp, mp, attack, defence, speed, and gold. WASD or the arrows move, and movement is axis-aligned with a tile collision test on the leading edge so the player slides along walls. E interacts with the nearest interactable within 40 px in the facing direction: an NPC, a chest, a shrine, a shop door, an inn door. I opens the inventory, Q the quest log, Escape the pause menu. The player is drawn as a small hooded figure with a two-frame walk cycle drawn in code.

CAMERA: follows the player, clamped to the area's edges, and everything drawn in the world passes through the camera offset. The canvas is fixed at 960×540 and scales to fit the window keeping aspect.

COLLISION: a tile is solid or not; NPCs are solid; the player cannot leave the area except through an exit. One function answers whether a pixel rectangle overlaps any solid tile, and everything moving uses it.

NPC: a record — id, name, area id, tile position, colour, a sprite variant index, a dialogue tree id, and an optional shop id. The roster the generator places: Elder Hesper in the elder's house (gives the main quest), Brannoc the innkeeper in the inn, Tilda the shopkeeper in the shop, Father Ambrose in the chapel, a wandering child named Pip on the plaza who walks a small random patrol, Old Rook the hermit at the Greenwood shrine, and Sarn the miner just inside the Old Mine's first room. NPCs face the player when talked to. An NPC's tile is chosen by the generator inside the building or clearing it belongs to.

DIALOGUE: a tree is a list of nodes; a node is a record — id, speaker, text, a list of choices, and an optional condition. A condition is a record naming a quest id and a required stage, or an item id the player must hold, or a flag id. A choice is a record — label, next node id, an optional quest stage it advances to, an optional item it grants, an optional flag it sets, and an optional condition to be shown at all. The engine starts at the tree's root, picks the first node whose condition holds, shows its text and its visible choices, and follows the chosen next id until a node with no choices ends the talk. Every NPC has a tree of at least three nodes and every NPC says something different at least once depending on quest stage. All dialogue text lives in one data file, written so that no string contains an unescaped quote of its own kind — use double-quoted strings for lines with apostrophes. The dialogue box is a wooden panel across the bottom of the screen with the speaker's name, the line typed out at 40 characters per second (any key finishes it), and choices navigated with up/down and Enter or by click.

ITEM: a record — id, name, kind (consumable, weapon, armour, key, quest), description, value in gold, and an effect record. A consumable's effect names a stat and an amount (potion: hp +30, ether: mp +15, antidote: cures poison). A weapon's effect is an attack bonus; armour's is a defence bonus. The catalogue: potion, hi-potion (hp +80), ether, antidote, rusty sword (+3), iron sword (+7), miner's pick (+5, speed +2), leather vest (+2), chain mail (+5), the mine key, the elder's letter, the founder's seal.

INVENTORY: a list of item ids with counts, and the gold. Opened with I as a panel listing items grouped by kind, with the selected item's description and, for a consumable, Use; for equipment, Equip. Picking up an item from a chest or a battle shows a toast naming it.

EQUIPMENT: two slots, weapon and armour, holding item ids or nothing; the player's effective attack and defence are base stats plus the equipped bonuses, computed by one function used everywhere attack and defence are read.

ENEMY: a record — id, name, max hp, attack, defence, speed, xp reward, gold reward, a drop table, a colour and shape for the code-drawn sprite, and a list of moves. A move is a record — name, mp cost, a power multiplier, and an optional status it inflicts (poison, which deals 5 per turn for three turns). The bestiary: forest wolf, giant spider (poisons), bandit (can steal 10 gold), cave bat, rock crawler, mine ghoul, and the boss Grelthak the Deep One (three moves, 200 hp, a claw, a poison breath, and a roar that lowers the player's defence for two turns). A drop table is a list of item id and chance pairs.

ENCOUNTER: each area with a table has a per-step encounter chance (forest 4%, mine 6%, road 3%, checked once per tile moved). A table is a list of enemy groups with weights; a group is one to three enemy ids. The boss trigger tile in the mine starts the boss battle once; after the boss falls the tile is inert and a flag records it.

BATTLE: turn-based on a separate screen with the current area's palette as backdrop, the player's stat panel bottom-left, the enemy group drawn across the middle, and a command menu: Attack, Skill, Item, Flee. Turn order each round is by speed, highest first. Damage is attacker's attack × move power minus the defender's defence, minimum 1, with a ±15% roll; a 10% chance to crit for 1.5×. Skills the player learns by level: Slash at level 1 (no cost, power 1.0), Fire Bolt at level 3 (5 mp, power 1.8), Heal at level 5 (6 mp, restores 40 hp), Whirlwind at level 8 (10 mp, hits every enemy at 1.2). Flee succeeds with 50% plus 5% per point of speed the player has over the fastest enemy, never in the boss battle. Every action prints one line in a battle log panel and plays an effect: a white flash on a hit, a red shake on the player taking damage, a shrinking fade on an enemy's death. The battle ends with victory (xp and gold summed from the group, drops rolled, a results panel), defeat (the game over screen), or a flee back to the map. Poison persists across turns and ticks once per round.

QUEST: a record — id, title, a list of stages each with a description, and the current stage index. The main quest, "The Founder's Seal": stage 0 given by Elder Hesper (the seal that keeps the town's charter was taken into the Old Mine; bring it back); stage 1 talk to Sarn the miner, who gives the mine key once the player carries the elder's letter; stage 2 defeat Grelthak; stage 3 open the chest behind the boss, which holds the founder's seal; stage 4 return the seal to Hesper for the ending. A side quest, "Rook's Remedy": Old Rook at the shrine wants an antidote; bringing one rewards the miner's pick and 50 gold. A side quest, "Pip's Lost Cat": Pip's cat is at the Hollow Road tower; finding it (interacting with a cat NPC placed inside the tower room) and returning rewards 80 gold and a hi-potion. The quest log opens with Q and lists each quest's title and current stage description.

SHOP: Tilda's shop sells potion 20, ether 30, antidote 25, iron sword 150, chain mail 200; it buys anything at half value. A buy and sell panel with the player's gold shown and a refusal line when gold is short. The inn heals hp and mp to full for 15 gold and saves the game.

PROGRESSION: xp to the next level is 20 × level squared; on a level-up max hp +8, max mp +4, attack +2, defence +1, speed +1, hp and mp refilled, and a fanfare with a panel listing the gains. Every skill unlock is announced in that panel.

SAVE: one save slot in localStorage holding the world seed, the player record, the area id and position, the inventory and equipment, every quest's stage, and the flag set. Saving happens at the inn and at a shrine; the title screen offers Continue when a save exists. Loading regenerates the areas from the seed and restores the rest.

SCREENS: title (name, a short setup paragraph, New Game, Continue) → playing → inventory overlay → quest log overlay → dialogue → shop → battle → results → level-up → game over (Retry from the last save, Title) → ending (the seal returned, the town's charter kept, credits, Title). Escape from playing opens a pause menu with Resume, Save (when at an inn or shrine, otherwise greyed), Title. Every overlay can be closed with Escape.

AUDIO: generated with the Web Audio API — a looping town tune (a gentle major-key arpeggio), a forest tune (slower, minor), a mine drone with a low pulse, a road tune, a battle theme (faster, driving), and one-shot effects for a hit, a heal, a menu move, a chest, a level-up fanfare, and the encounter sting. The music id is on the area record and the battle switches to the battle theme and back.
