Build this first-person 3D castle dungeon crawler in which the player explores five generated floors, buys gear, fights monsters, and defeats the Castle Lord. It is made of these systems. Every number below is a given value to write into the code as-is; none of them needs checking or deriving.

SYSTEMS: WORLD STATE, CASTLE GENERATOR, PLAYER RECORD, ENEMY ROSTER, COMBAT SYSTEM, INVENTORY SYSTEM, SHOP SYSTEM, HEALTH SYSTEM, QUEST SYSTEM, CAMERA SYSTEM, INPUT SYSTEM, UI SYSTEM, SCREENS, AUDIO, ART.

WORLD STATE: The World State record is the single source of truth for the current floor and contains the fields seed, floor, rooms, corridors, doors, stairs, monsters, items, merchant, and objective. Each room record has id, center, width, depth, wallHeight, and doorIds. Each corridor record has id, fromRoom, toRoom, width, length, and angle. Each door record has id, roomA, roomB, position, and open. The stairs record has position and active. Each monster instance has id, type, position, health, state, and lastAttack. Each item instance has id, type, position, and taken. The merchant record has position, stockPotions, stockSwords, stockAmulets, and stockTorches. The objective record has floor, goal, target, and completed. Every door's open field is true, every item's taken field is false, every monster's state is idle, and the stairs active field is false at floor start. The CASTLE GENERATOR writes this record, and the CAMERA SYSTEM, COMBAT SYSTEM, QUEST SYSTEM, SHOP SYSTEM, and UI SYSTEM read it without keeping a second copy of geometry or entity state.

CASTLE GENERATOR: The CASTLE GENERATOR lays out every floor with a seeded generator and never uses a hand-written map or literal map. The floor seeds are 1434, 1531, 1628, 1725, and 1822 for floors 1 through 5. It places, in order, the start room, the stairs room on floors 1 through 4, the throne room on floor 5, the remaining rooms, corridors, doors, the merchant, item instances, and monster instances. Floor 1 has 9 rooms, 14 corridors, 10 monsters, 2 potions, 1 torch, and 1 merchant. Floor 2 has 11 rooms, 18 corridors, 14 monsters, 2 potions, 1 torch, and 1 merchant. Floor 3 has 13 rooms, 22 corridors, 18 monsters, 2 potions, 2 torches, 1 silver amulet, and 1 merchant. Floor 4 has 15 rooms, 26 corridors, 22 monsters, 3 potions, 2 torches, and 1 merchant. Floor 5 has 17 rooms, 30 corridors, 26 monsters, 3 potions, 2 torches, and 1 merchant. Rooms are rectangles with width 6 to 12 and depth 6 to 12, wallHeight 3.2; corridors have width 2.2; doors have width 1.4. The generator chooses enemy types by floor: floor 1 uses 6 slimes and 4 bats, floor 2 uses 6 slimes, 4 bats, and 4 skeletons, floor 3 uses 4 slimes, 4 bats, 6 skeletons, and 4 knights, floor 4 uses 4 bats, 8 skeletons, and 10 knights, and floor 5 uses 6 bats, 8 skeletons, 11 knights, and the Castle Lord. It places the merchant in the start room, potions in random non-start rooms, torches in random non-start rooms, the silver amulet on floor 3 in a random non-start room, and monsters in random rooms other than the start room and, on floor 5, the throne room except the Castle Lord. The result must pass a verifier: the start room is connected to the stairs room through open doors, the throne room is reachable on floor 5, no two room rectangles overlap, no corridor crosses a room interior except through a door, no monster or item is inside a wall, and the floor contains at least the listed minimum counts; if any check fails, re-roll with the next seed.

PLAYER RECORD: The Player Record is the single source of truth for the player and contains position, yaw, pitch, health, maxHealth, gold, score, weapon, inventory, and attackCooldown. The inventory record contains potionCount, ironSword, silverAmulet, and torchCount. The player starts with health 100, maxHealth 100, gold 40, score 0, weapon woodenSword, potionCount 1, ironSword false, silverAmulet false, torchCount 0, and attackCooldown 0. The PLAYER RECORD is read by the COMBAT SYSTEM, HEALTH SYSTEM, INVENTORY SYSTEM, SHOP SYSTEM, CAMERA SYSTEM, and UI SYSTEM, so no other system stores a second copy of player position, health, gold, score, or carried items.

ENEMY ROSTER: The Enemy Roster is the single source of truth for enemy definitions and contains the fields type, maxHealth, speed, damage, attackRange, aggroRange, attackCooldown, score, and gold. The roster lists slime: maxHealth 24, speed 1.8, damage 6, attackRange 1.2, aggroRange 6, attackCooldown 1.0, score 10, gold 5; bat: maxHealth 16, speed 3.4, damage 5, attackRange 1.0, aggroRange 8, attackCooldown 0.8, score 15, gold 8; skeleton: maxHealth 42, speed 2.4, damage 10, attackRange 1.6, aggroRange 7, attackCooldown 1.2, score 25, gold 15; knight: maxHealth 70, speed 2.0, damage 16, attackRange 1.8, aggroRange 9, attackCooldown 1.4, score 45, gold 30; Castle Lord: maxHealth 240, speed 2.6, damage 22, attackRange 2.0, aggroRange 10, attackCooldown 1.6, score 300, gold 200. Monster instances in the World State store only type, position, health, state, and lastAttack, and the COMBAT SYSTEM reads the Enemy Roster for every stat.

COMBAT SYSTEM: The COMBAT SYSTEM reads the Player Record, World State, and Enemy Roster. The player's attack has range 1.8, arc 70 degrees, cooldown 0.45 seconds, and damage 12 when the weapon is woodenSword or 18 when the weapon is ironSword. An attack is a hit when the attackCooldown is 0, the target is inside the range and arc, and a line-of-sight ray from the player to the target does not pass through a wall. A hit reduces the monster's health by the weapon damage, increases the player's score by the roster score, and sets the player's attackCooldown to 0.45. If monster health reaches 0, the monster state becomes dead, the World State removes it after a 0.4 second death animation, and the player gains the roster gold. Enemies are idle, chase, or dead. An idle enemy becomes chase when the player is inside its aggroRange and the line-of-sight ray is clear. A chase enemy moves along the corridor path to the player at its roster speed, stops at its attackRange, and attacks when its lastAttack timer has passed its attackCooldown. An enemy attack reduces player health by its roster damage. If player health reaches 0, the SCREENS state becomes game over.

INVENTORY SYSTEM: The INVENTORY SYSTEM owns the Item Roster and the player's inventory record. The Item Roster contains type, effect, price, and stack. It lists health potion: heals 35, price 25, stack 9; iron sword: weapon damage 18, price 80, stack 1; silver amulet: maxHealth 120, price 120, stack 1; torch: light radius 8 for 1, 9 for 2, 10 for 3, price 20, stack 3. The player's inventory record in the Player Record contains potionCount, ironSword, silverAmulet, and torchCount. The player touches an item when within 0.8 meters of the item position. When the player touches a potion and potionCount is below 9, the item's taken field becomes true and potionCount increases by 1. When the player touches a torch and torchCount is below 3, the item's taken field becomes true and torchCount increases by 1. When the player touches a silver amulet and silverAmulet is false, the item's taken field becomes true and silverAmulet becomes true. Using a potion when health is below maxHealth increases health by 35 and reduces potionCount by 1. Buying the iron sword sets ironSword true and changes weapon to ironSword. Buying the silver amulet sets silverAmulet true and changes maxHealth to 120. The wooden sword is always owned and cannot be bought.

SHOP SYSTEM: The SHOP SYSTEM reads the merchant record in the World State and the Item Roster. The merchant appears in the start room of every floor at a fixed position and has stockPotions 5, stockSwords 1, stockAmulets 1, and stockTorches 2. The player opens the shop by pressing F or the touch interact button while facing the merchant within 2.0 meters. The shop shows the Item Roster prices for health potion, iron sword, silver amulet, and torch. A purchase is allowed when the player has enough gold and the merchant has stock. A purchase reduces player gold by the price, reduces the matching merchant stock by 1, and increases the matching inventory field by 1. If the player already has the iron sword or silver amulet, the purchase is denied. The shop cannot sell the wooden sword, which is always owned.

HEALTH SYSTEM: The HEALTH SYSTEM reads the Player Record and the INVENTORY SYSTEM. Player health starts at 100 and maxHealth is 100, or 120 when the silver amulet is owned. Health never exceeds maxHealth. Enemy attacks reduce health by the enemy damage from the Enemy Roster. Using a potion heals 35. If health reaches 0, the player dies and the SCREENS state becomes game over. At the start of each new floor, the player's health is set to 80.

QUEST SYSTEM: The QUEST SYSTEM reads the objective record in the World State. On floors 1 through 4, the goal is clear the floor, meaning every monster instance on that floor is dead, and the target is the stairs. The stairs are inactive until all monsters are dead. The player touches the stairs when the player position is within 0.8 meters of the stairs position. When the player touches the active stairs, the QUEST SYSTEM sets completed true, the floor increases by 1, the player position is set to the start room center of the next floor, and health is set to 80. On floor 5, the goal is defeat the Castle Lord, the target is the Castle Lord, and the stairs do not exist. When the Castle Lord's health reaches 0, the QUEST SYSTEM sets completed true and SCREENS becomes victory. The UI SYSTEM reads this record to display the current objective.

CAMERA SYSTEM: The CAMERA SYSTEM reads the Player Record and World State. It is first-person, with eye height 1.6, field of view 75, mouse sensitivity 0.0022 radians per pixel, and pitch limits -75 degrees and +75 degrees. The camera position is the player position plus eye height, and the camera direction is the player yaw and pitch. It renders rooms, corridors, doors, stairs, monsters, items, and the merchant as 3D boxes and billboard sprites using the World State. It draws a light cone around the player with radius 5, or radius 8, 9, or 10 for torchCount 1, 2, or 3, and darkens everything outside that radius. It draws the first-person wooden sword sprite at the bottom center when the weapon is woodenSword and the first-person iron sword sprite when the weapon is ironSword.

INPUT SYSTEM: The INPUT SYSTEM reads the Screen State record to know which keys are active and binds keyboard and touch controls. W or ArrowUp moves forward, S or ArrowDown moves backward, A or ArrowLeft strafes left, D or ArrowRight strafes right, Q turns left, E turns right, Space attacks, Shift sprints, Tab opens the inventory, F interacts with the merchant, and Escape toggles pause. Mouse movement while pointer is locked changes yaw and pitch. The player has a collision radius 0.4 and cannot move through walls. Touch controls use a left virtual joystick for forward, back, left, and right movement, a right drag area for yaw and pitch, an attack button at the bottom right, an interact button at the bottom left, and a pause button at the top right. Sprint speed is 6.0 m/s and walk speed is 4.2 m/s.

UI SYSTEM: The UI SYSTEM reads the Player Record, World State, Enemy Roster, Item Roster, and Screen State. It draws a health bar at the bottom left with width 220 pixels and height 18 pixels, a gold counter at the bottom right, a score counter below the gold, a floor label at the top left, an objective label at the top center, an attack cooldown ring around the crosshair, a crosshair in the center, and a damage flash at the screen edges when the player is hit. The inventory screen shows potionCount, ironSword, silverAmulet, and torchCount with their prices from the Item Roster. The shop screen shows merchant stock and prices. The UI never changes player or world state except by calling the INVENTORY SYSTEM or SHOP SYSTEM actions.

SCREENS: The Screen State record is the source of truth for the current screen and contains state; the UI SYSTEM, INPUT SYSTEM, and AUDIO SYSTEM read it. The screens are a state machine, and state is title, game, paused, game over, or victory. Title shows the castle gate title scene, the game name, a start button, and a controls list; pressing Enter or tapping start leads to game. Game shows the 3D dungeon and HUD; Escape leads to paused. Paused shows resume, restart, and quit; resume leads to game, restart leads to game with a new seed, quit leads to title. Game over shows the castle gate game over scene, the score, and retry or title; retry leads to game with a new seed, title leads to title. Victory shows the throne room victory scene, the final score, and title; title leads to title. Escape in title does nothing, Escape in game opens paused, Escape in paused returns to game, Escape in game over or victory leads to title.

AUDIO: The AUDIO SYSTEM reads the Screen State record and generates all sound with the Web Audio API. It plays a footstep sound every 0.5 seconds while the player moves, a swing sound when the player attacks, a hit sound when the player damages a monster, a monster hit sound when a monster attacks the player, a monster death sound when a monster reaches 0 health, a pickup sound when an item is taken, a potion sound when a potion is used, a purchase sound when a purchase succeeds, a boss roar sound when the Castle Lord enters chase state, a victory sting when the Castle Lord dies, and a game over sting when player health reaches 0. An ambient drone plays continuously during game and stops on title, game over, and victory.

ART: Ask for a castle gate title scene as a scene, a castle gate game over scene as a scene, a throne room victory scene as a scene, a stone wall tile as a tile, a stone floor tile as a tile, a wooden door tile as a tile, a stone stair tile as a tile, a slime enemy sprite as a sprite, a bat enemy sprite as a sprite, a skeleton enemy sprite as a sprite, a knight enemy sprite as a sprite, a Castle Lord boss sprite as a sprite, a gate merchant sprite as a sprite, a health potion sprite as a sprite, a torch item sprite as a sprite, a silver amulet sprite as a sprite, an iron sword item sprite as a sprite, a first-person wooden sword sprite as a sprite, and a first-person iron sword sprite as a sprite. Each image holds one subject. Everything else — wall geometry, corridor geometry, door geometry, stair geometry, player capsule, enemy health bars, damage numbers, HUD, menus, shadows, fog, light cone, crosshair, touch controls — is drawn in code.
